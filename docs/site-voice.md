# Site voice

This guide is for page headings, introductions, project summaries, calls to action, metadata, and other short copy
around the site. Articles and dated `/now` entries should keep the voice they were written in.

## What sounds like Stefan

- Direct, first-person sentences
- Specific details instead of broad themes
- Contractions and everyday words
- A little humor when it comes naturally
- Admitting when something is new, unfinished, or still being learned

Good examples already on the site:

- “I build systems for a living and escape plans for fun.”
- “Most of these went through my ears, not my eyes. A book is a book.”
- “The first one was pretty awesome, so why not?”

## Constructions to question

These are not banned. They are signs to read the sentence again.

- An em dash that turns a plain sentence into a polished lesson
- Three abstract nouns or gerunds in a row
- “Not this, but that” reversals that sound ready for a quote card
- Corporate phrases such as “operate in ambiguity” or “automate and streamline”
- Vague phrases such as “intentional life design,” “cross-domain thinking,” “learning in public,” “along the way,”
  or “things worth sharing”
- Claims that could be pasted onto another developer's site without changing a word

## Write the concrete version first

Start by answering the ordinary question.

- **Page intro:** What is on this page?
- **Project:** Why did I build it, and what came out of it?
- **Talk:** Where did I give it, and what was it actually about?
- **Newsletter:** What do I write about, and how often does it show up?
- **About copy:** What have I actually done, and what am I doing now?

Shorten that answer after it is specific. Do not turn it into a slogan just because it sits under a large heading.

## Before publishing

- Would I say this out loud to someone I met at a conference?
- Does it name something concrete?
- Is the last clause trying too hard to teach a lesson?
- Have I used the same phrase elsewhere on the site?
- Does the metadata match the visible copy?
- Can any sentence be deleted without losing useful information?
