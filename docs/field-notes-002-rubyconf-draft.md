# Field Notes 002: Show Up Before You Feel Ready

**Subject:** A few months into Ruby, I gave a talk at RubyConf

**Preview text:** I showed up knowing mostly syntax and left ready to volunteer again.

Hey,

I'm finally back from RubyConf 2026 in Las Vegas.

It was my first Ruby conference, my first Ruby-related tech event, and only my second tech conference ever. Toward the end, I had this surprisingly emotional feeling that I did not want it to end. I thought that was pretty amazing.

The funny part is that I went in knowing mostly Ruby syntax. I only made it onto The Odin Project's Ruby on Rails path a couple of months ago. Walking into a conference full of people who build with Ruby for a living could have been intimidating, and sometimes it was.

But I went because I wanted to be part of the community, not because I had already earned a place in it.

I volunteered and helped wherever I was needed. Then I got picked to give my first lightning talk and ended up closing out the night.

The talk was not about some clever Ruby technique. I am definitely not the person for that talk yet. It was about showing up before I felt ready.

The point was simple:

> Participation is not a reward for expertise.

I used to think the order was learn, build, then participate. RubyConf showed me those things can happen together.

That became real at the conference hack space. Before the trip, I had reached out to one of the RubyEvents maintainers who streams on Twitch and spent some time learning the codebase. At the conference, I got to work with the people who maintain it and had [my first Ruby-related pull request merged](https://github.com/rubyevents/rubyevents/pull/1940).

It updated RubyConf involvement and speaker information. It was not a huge Ruby feature, but it was useful, it was merged, and it gave me a real starting point in open source.

The human side was even better. I met people I actually got to know instead of trying to collect 50 or 60 surface-level connections. We talked about what we were building, what life looked like outside of tech, and what we were looking forward to next.

I came home still learning Ruby and still knowing how much I do not know. I also came home already wanting to volunteer again.

The first one was pretty awesome, so why not?
